# Ember & Stone — Teach D&D Curriculum

Ember & Stone is the teaching channel for the Hearthford Level 1–20 one-shot ladder.

## Core model

Each adventure must do two jobs:

1. Be a complete, fun, standalone D&D one-shot.
2. Teach specific D&D concepts through play.

The YouTube lesson should use the same adventure, encounters, maps, NPCs, and decisions as concrete examples rather than teaching rules in a vacuum.

## Adventure → lesson contract

Every ladder adventure should identify:

- **Player lessons** — rules and decisions players learn by experiencing the adventure.
- **DM lessons** — encounter design, adjudication, pacing, clue design, fail-forward, monster use, and campaign continuity demonstrated by the adventure.
- **Rules coverage** — saves, checks, damage types, conditions, movement, cover, concentration, reactions, rests/resources, hazards, spell interactions, and other RAW mechanics intentionally exercised.
- **Monster coverage** — monster families and tactical patterns introduced.
- **Adventure-design coverage** — clues, red herrings, traps, puzzles, BBEGs, twists, social pressure, exploration, timed objectives, alternate victories, environmental hazards, and consequences.
- **Edition notes** — 2014 and 2024 examples must remain separate.
- **Hearthford continuity** — recurring NPCs and consequences connect the lesson to an ongoing world.

## Ember & Stone video outputs

Each one-shot can support several videos:

- **Run This Adventure** — spoiler-light pitch and DM prep.
- **What This Adventure Teaches** — the intended D&D lesson.
- **DM Breakdown** — why the encounters, clues, red herrings, traps, twists, and fail-forward paths work.
- **Rules Lab** — one focused mechanic demonstrated using a scene from the adventure.
- **Monster Lab** — how to run a featured monster family or tactical pattern.
- **Player Lesson** — what players should understand about actions, saves, movement, teamwork, resources, or spell use.
- **After-Action Review** — alternate choices, missed clues, failure states, and how different parties could have changed the outcome.

One adventure does not need all seven videos. Use the curriculum ledger to choose the lessons with the highest educational value.

## 1–20 learning arc

- **Levels 1–4: Foundations** — ability checks, attack rolls, basic saves, simple hazards, action economy, movement, conditions, social checks, clue redundancy, basic traps and puzzles.
- **Levels 5–8: Expansion** — Extra Attack / stronger spell tiers, flight and mobility, control, resistance/vulnerability, multi-objective encounters, stronger hazards, complex social situations, monster tactics, layered mysteries.
- **Levels 9–12: Advanced play** — teleportation, stronger utility magic, planar effects, sophisticated counterplay, battlefield control, complex BBEGs, lairs, multi-stage objectives, resource denial, stronger condition interactions.
- **Levels 13–16: High-level play** — powerful movement and information magic, major immunities/resistances, legendary-style pressure, environmental transformation, large-area threats, resurrection/banishment/teleportation implications.
- **Levels 17–20: Epic play** — reality-changing magic, extreme mobility, top-tier defenses, world-scale stakes, legendary and mythic patterns, multiple simultaneous objectives, and designing encounters that remain meaningful when characters have extraordinary tools.

## Design rule

Do not add a mechanic merely to tick a curriculum box. The mechanic must make sense in the fiction and produce a useful play decision.

When several later adventures could teach the same rule, prefer the one that naturally demonstrates it and use the remaining adventure slots to cover missing parts of D&D.

## Channel identity

**Ember & Stone teaches D&D by playing D&D.**

The Hearthford adventures are the practical curriculum: recurring NPCs, escalating character levels, increasing mechanical complexity, and lessons that build on what earlier adventures taught.
