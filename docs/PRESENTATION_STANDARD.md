# Read-Aloud and Card Presentation Standard

This file is authoritative for the spoken/table-facing layer of One-Shot Forge adventures.

## Core principle

The DM should not have to invent the world between mechanics. The package supplies enough sensory description, NPC voice, creature behavior, and scene transitions that the DM can read, pause, ask the players what they do, and continue.

## Scene order contract

Every adventure has one explicit primary scene spine.

Each scene must define:
- purpose;
- location;
- 2–4 sentence read-aloud;
- ordered Run Steps;
- the exact condition that advances the scene;
- explicit exit target(s);
- branch descriptions only when player choice creates a genuinely different consequence.

The runner must present scenes in story order. A scene cannot depend on a later scene for information needed to run it now.

## Read-aloud contract

Read-aloud is spoken to players and must:
1. orient them physically;
2. include at least two useful sensory details when appropriate;
3. put the actionable person/object/problem in view;
4. avoid hidden information or DM-only conclusions;
5. end with tension, motion, dialogue, or a clear prompt.

Read-aloud should sound natural aloud. Prefer concrete nouns and verbs over lore exposition.

## Location description contract

Every important room/location should establish:
- size or spatial feel;
- light/weather/temperature when relevant;
- one strong sound;
- one strong smell or tactile detail when relevant;
- obvious exits;
- obvious interactable objects;
- what changes when danger begins.

Combat locations additionally need useful distances, cover/terrain, entry points, and evacuation routes.

## NPC contract

Every important NPC needs:
- one-sentence visual description;
- role in the current scene;
- immediate want;
- what they know;
- what they hide, if anything;
- voice direction;
- up to three strong dialogue lines that can be read verbatim;
- reaction to agreement, refusal, accusation, and danger when relevant.

Dialogue must sound like that NPC, not like exposition wearing a name tag. Short statements are preferred over speeches.

## Monster/creature contract

Every monster card needs:
- strong visual description before mechanics;
- what it wants right now;
- how it behaves before combat;
- combat stats on the same card;
- special state/rider written in runnable language;
- tactics in priority order;
- noncombat bypass or de-escalation when one exists;
- sensory cues showing injury, fear, hunger, rage, or surrender.

Creatures do not need spoken dialogue. Their sounds and body language are their dialogue.

## Card contract

Printable NPC/monster cards must be readable at the table:
- front: art + title + one clear identity line;
- back: role/location, up to three dialogue/voice lines, compact rules/stats, and DM notes;
- do not exceed the card validator's readability limits;
- all card facts must match the adventure and Quick Run exactly.

## Transition contract

The end of one core scene should naturally create the beginning of the next.

Examples:
- debate interrupted by the father crashing into town;
- father consequence exposes the mother's scream;
- wounded village creates Elmorrow's attempt to leave;
- Elmorrow reveal returns the table to the egg;
- final egg choice opens exactly one consequence scene.

A transition should never feel like the DM is changing chapters arbitrarily.

## AI flesh contract

AI may improve:
- dialogue;
- sensory description;
- read-aloud;
- transitions;
- cosmetic names/titles when permitted.

AI may not change bones:
- scene order;
- exits;
- clues;
- motives;
- allegiances;
- DC/AC/HP/damage;
- encounter resolution;
- finale/outcome state.

Generated flesh is invalid if it contradicts the deterministic adventure package.

## Release gate

Before release, read the adventure aloud in scene order. If the DM must stop and invent:
- what a place looks/sounds/smells like;
- what an important NPC says;
- what a monster is trying to do;
- why the next scene starts;
then the presentation layer is incomplete.
