# From Zero to Hero Standard

**Status: Level-0 house-rule contract for One-Shot Forge**

From Zero to Hero is a pre-Level-1 origin mini-campaign for 5E characters. It is deliberately isolated from normal 2014/2024 rules. It exists to let players discover a character through play, then graduate into a legal Level-1 character for the selected edition.

## 1. Core promise

Characters begin around age 12, or the ancestry/species-equivalent adolescent life stage, as ordinary members of one persistent community. They have no class and no class features. Across seven yearly mini-adventures, ages 12 through 18, the characters build their ability array, relationships, training, reputation, mentor ties, possessions, heirloom, and reason for adventuring.

The custom Level-0 sheet answers **Who am I becoming?**

The normal Level-1 through Level-20 sheet answers **What can I do as an adventurer?**

## 2. Starting character

Every Level-0 character begins with:

- STR 9
- DEX 9
- CON 9
- INT 9
- WIS 9
- CHA 9
- Level 0
- proficiency bonus +2 for the limited checks that explicitly grant proficiency
- no class, subclass, class features, spellcasting, Fighting Style, Rage, Sneak Attack, Channel Divinity, or other class package
- ordinary clothing and only possessions justified by family/home
- no adventuring equipment package
- no magic item unless specifically earned by story

Species/ancestry identity exists at Level 0, but edition-specific mechanical traits are applied only by the certified graduation path.

## 3. Ability development budget

Starting all six scores at 9 consumes the equivalent of 6 points of a normal 27-point-buy array. From Zero to Hero therefore awards exactly **21 Development Points** across the complete origin campaign.

There are seven yearly mini-adventures. Each character has exactly three Development Opportunities in each chapter.

**7 chapters × 3 opportunities = 21 Development Points.**

A Development Point is assigned to the ability the player actually relied on in the scene.

| Current score | Development Points required for next score |
| --- | ---: |
| 9 → 10 | 1 |
| 10 → 11 | 1 |
| 11 → 12 | 1 |
| 12 → 13 | 1 |
| 13 → 14 | 2 |
| 14 → 15 | 2 |

15 is the Level-0 pre-origin cap. A player may not assign a Development Point to an ability already at 15; the character must solve the next growth opportunity in another way.

The resulting array remains within the normal point-buy envelope before edition-specific origin adjustments.

Because a score of 13 or 14 costs two Development Points to raise, a character can finish age 18 with one or more incomplete marks. Those marks are **not lost**. Before graduation, a no-roll **Final Reflection** step pools any incomplete marks and spends the remainder of the earned budget until the pre-origin array equals exactly the normal **27-point point-buy budget**, with no score above 15. This prevents different approach patterns from accidentally producing weaker graduates. Final Reflection adds no new points; it only converts already-earned development into a complete legal base array.

## 4. Development follows behavior

The player never earns Strength because they declared an intention to become a Fighter. The chosen approach determines the ability.

- STR — force, lifting, wrestling, breaking, hauling
- DEX — balance, stealth, precision, climbing, manual finesse
- CON — endurance, holding out, resisting hardship, protecting through stamina
- INT — study, deduction, engineering, recalling or learning
- WIS — noticing, empathy, judgment, tracking, survival
- CHA — persuasion, leadership, deception, performance, intimidation

Every Growth Beat must support multiple plausible approaches. No scene has one correct ability.

## 5. Level-0 developmental checks

Developmental checks keep the normal 5E resolution procedure:

1. roll the d20;
2. add the relevant ability modifier and only legitimately earned proficiency/circumstantial modifiers;
3. compare the total to the authored DC;
4. total meeting/exceeding the DC succeeds; a lower total fails.

From Zero to Hero adds one isolated **narrative critical tier**:

- if a successful check also rolled a natural 20, it is a **critical success**;
- if a failed check also rolled a natural 1, it is a **critical failure**;
- a natural 20 does **not** turn a failed total into a success;
- a natural 1 does **not** turn a successful total into a failure.

This is intentionally closer to normal 2014/2024 resolution. The extra "critical" label is still a Level-0 house rule and disappears at Level 1.

### Equal Growth rule

Every genuine Development Opportunity grants **exactly one Development Point** for the chosen approach whether the outcome is:

- critical success;
- success;
- failure; or
- critical failure.

There is **no growth bonus for success** and **no growth penalty for failure**. Luck changes the fiction, not permanent character power.

An outcome may never directly grant or remove:
- Development Points;
- ability-score points;
- proficiency;
- a feat;
- a spell;
- a class feature;
- a permanent magic-item property;
- graduation eligibility.

Ordinary success/failure changes the immediate fictional result or cost. A critical result may improve or worsen that fictional consequence by **one tier**. A critical success can save an extra resource, improve a relationship, reveal extra information, or create one temporary local edge. A critical failure can create a scar, damaged relationship, lost resource, clock pressure, or harder follow-up. Neither may create a permanent build-power gap.

Failure must fail forward and may not brick the campaign or arbitrarily kill a child character.

## 5A. Training, familiarity, and proficiency

Level-0 training is recorded as **familiarity**, not automatic 5E proficiency.

A character can repeatedly practice Athletics, smithing, a weapon, a language, herbalism, arcane study, and so on. Those entries document who the character is becoming and may guide graduation choices.

In default **RAW Graduation** mode, familiarity adds no proficiency bonus by itself. At Level 1 it becomes mechanical only when a legal option from the selected edition grants it:
- 2014 race/background/class choices;
- 2024 species/background/class choices.

If the legal Level-1 package does not grant that proficiency, the familiarity remains narrative history. This prevents the Level-0 campaign from silently adding extra proficiencies to an otherwise RAW character.

## 6. Growing-up sheet

The Level-0 sheet tracks:

- name
- ancestry/species
- chronological age
- ancestry-equivalent life stage
- family/guardians
- home/community
- childhood work/chores
- values/personality
- fears
- bonds
- friend and rival
- **What I am striving to become**
- all six scores and unspent progress toward the next point
- learned skill/tool/weapon/language familiarities
- training
- injuries/scars
- relationships
- reputation
- possessions
- important choices
- mentor candidates and mentor progress
- heirloom identity/state
- yearly milestone history
- discovered calling

Aspiration is controlled by the player and may change at any time. It is guidance, never a class lock.

## 7. Village contract

The starting village is a persistent campaign character.

It needs:
- recurring family/guardians
- peers, friends, rivals
- martial, arcane, divine, primal, and expert/social mentor paths
- authority figure
- healer
- crafter
- merchant/innkeeper
- troublemaker
- outsider
- evolving mystery NPC

Important NPCs need appearance, voice, wants, fears, secret, relationship state, what they can teach/provide, and how they change over the years.

The village must visibly evolve: marriages, deaths, departures, businesses, repairs, damage, promotions, aging mentors, grudges, and consequences of player choices.

## 8. Mini-adventure contract

Each yearly mini-adventure requires:
- active objective
- three Development Opportunities per character
- explicit DCs
- all four outcome bands
- multiple ability approaches
- at least one persistent NPC relationship beat
- at least one meaningful choice/consequence
- visible pressure/clock when useful
- fail-forward resolution
- concrete activity, not conversation alone

Combat is sparse. When combat occurs, it has a job beyond reducing HP and provides noncombatant/support roles for characters who are not developing martially.

## 9. Graduation: 2014

Level-0 produces the **pre-origin base ability array**.

For 2014 graduation:
1. freeze the earned base array;
2. apply the selected legal 2014 species/race ability adjustments and traits;
3. apply the legal 2014 background proficiencies/equipment/features;
4. apply the selected certified Level-1 class package;
5. carry forward story identity, relationships, mentor, scars, reputation, and eligible learned proficiencies only where the graduation rules explicitly allow them;
6. do not import any 2024 origin feat/background mechanics.

## 10. Graduation: 2024

Level-0 produces the **pre-origin base ability array**.

For 2024 graduation:
1. freeze the earned base array;
2. apply the selected legal 2024 background ability-score increases;
3. apply the selected legal 2024 origin feat and background proficiencies/equipment;
4. apply legal species traits;
5. apply the selected certified Level-1 class package;
6. carry forward story identity, relationships, mentor, scars, reputation, and eligible learned proficiencies only where the graduation rules explicitly allow them;
7. do not import 2014 race/background mechanics.

## 11. RAW Graduation and optional Origin Legacy

**RAW Graduation is the default.**

In RAW Graduation mode:
- no extra feat is granted;
- no extra ASI is granted;
- no extra proficiency is granted;
- no class feature or spell is granted early;
- an heirloom remains mundane/narrative unless the selected ruleset legally grants the item;
- a mentor is a narrative contact and provides no automatic numerical bonus.

A separate **Heroic Legacy** switch may later enable a bounded house-rule reward such as a dormant/scaling heirloom. That mode must be visibly labeled non-RAW and is off by default.

A bonus feat is never a default Level-0 reward. This is especially important in 2024, where backgrounds already provide an Origin feat.

Mentors/contacts are narrative resources, not unlimited problem solvers.

## 12. Safety and tone

The characters begin as adolescents. Early chapters should use age-appropriate stakes: family, community, accidents, weather, animals, mysteries, competitions, chores, rescue, social conflict, and limited danger.

Consequences can matter without making child death the normal failure state. Stakes and combat danger increase as the characters age.

## 13. Release gate

From Zero to Hero is not production-ready until:
- the data contract validates;
- all seven chapters exist;
- ages run 12 through 18;
- exactly 21 Development Opportunities exist;
- every Growth Beat has DC and four outcome bands;
- every Growth Beat supports all six ability approaches or documents why one is impossible;
- scores cannot exceed 15 before graduation;
- the normal total-vs-DC result is resolved before any Level-0 critical story tier;
- all four roll outcomes award exactly one Development Point;
- no roll outcome can alter permanent build power;
- incomplete marks are converted by Final Reflection so the base array audits to exactly 27 point-buy points;
- familiarity cannot silently become proficiency;
- RAW Graduation is the default and optional Legacy rewards are off by default;
- 2014 and 2024 graduation paths are isolated;
- the custom sheet persists and prints;
- no Level-0 rule leaks into ordinary adventure checks.
