# From Zero to Hero Standard

**Status: Level-0 house-rule contract for One-Shot Forge**

From Zero to Hero is a pre-Level-1 origin mini-campaign for 5E characters. It is deliberately isolated from normal 2014/2024 rules. It exists to let players discover a character through play, then graduate into a legal Level-1 character for the selected edition.

## 1. Core promise

Characters begin around age 12, or the ancestry/species-equivalent adolescent life stage, as ordinary members of one persistent community. They have no class and no class features. Across seven yearly mini-adventures, ages 12 through 18, the characters build their ability array, relationships, training, reputation, mentor ties, possessions, heirloom, and reason for adventuring.

The custom Level-0 sheet answers **Who am I becoming?**

The normal Level-1 through Level-20 sheet answers **What can I do as an adventurer?**

## 2. Starting character

Every Level-0 character begins with:

- STR 9
- DEX 9
- CON 9
- INT 9
- WIS 9
- CHA 9
- Level 0
- proficiency bonus +2 for the limited checks that explicitly grant proficiency
- no class, subclass, class features, spellcasting, Fighting Style, Rage, Sneak Attack, Channel Divinity, or other class package
- ordinary clothing and only possessions justified by family/home
- no adventuring equipment package
- no magic item unless specifically earned by story

Species/ancestry identity exists at Level 0, but edition-specific mechanical traits are applied only by the certified graduation path.

## 3. Development wallet and ability growth

Every Level-0 character begins with six 9s. On the normal 5E point-buy table, that starting array has a value of 6 points. The seven mini-adventures contain 21 Development Opportunities.

The player does **not** automatically apply growth to the ability they rolled. Growth enters a visible wallet and is spent between scenes whenever the player chooses.

Ability costs mirror the normal point-buy curve from the age-12 floor:

| Increase | Development cost |
| --- | ---: |
| 9 → 10 | 1 |
| 10 → 11 | 1 |
| 11 → 12 | 1 |
| 12 → 13 | 1 |
| 13 → 14 | 2 |
| 14 → 15 | 2 |

15 is the Level-0 pre-origin cap.

With no critical-success bonuses, resolving all 21 opportunities yields the equivalent of a normal 27-point budget: the starting six-point array plus 21 earned development points.

Critical successes can add bonus Development Points beyond that normal target. The base ability array has an absolute **30-point-buy-value ceiling**. If bonus points push the array above 27, it must be labeled **Heroic / house-rule ability generation**; no investment may raise it above 30. All 2014/2024 mechanics applied after the array is frozen remain edition-locked and RAW unless another house rule is explicitly enabled.

### 27-point graduation floor

Completing all 21 base Development Opportunities guarantees a **minimum final pre-origin point-buy value of 27**.

Failure and critical failure may redirect development into abilities the player did not intend to prioritize, but they may not reduce the total baseline development earned.

At age 18:
- graduation is blocked while the base array is below 27 point-buy value;
- remaining Lesson Points must be spent in their tagged abilities where possible;
- any Lesson Point blocked by the 15 cap may be converted to flexible growth;
- any partial invested progress that cannot complete the next score increase may be returned to the flexible wallet as **Maturity Settlement**;
- Maturity Settlement never creates new Development Points; it only prevents already-earned development from being stranded by the stepped point-buy costs;
- critical-success bonus growth may raise the final array above 27 but never above 30;
- once the array reaches 30, further Development Points may remain recorded but cannot create additional ability-score power.

## 4. Development follows behavior

The player never earns Strength because they declared an intention to become a Fighter. The chosen approach determines the ability.

- STR — force, lifting, wrestling, breaking, hauling
- DEX — balance, stealth, precision, climbing, manual finesse
- CON — endurance, holding out, resisting hardship, protecting through stamina
- INT — study, deduction, engineering, recalling or learning
- WIS — noticing, empathy, judgment, tracking, survival
- CHA — persuasion, leadership, deception, performance, intimidation

Every Growth Beat must support multiple plausible approaches. No scene has one correct ability.

## 5. Level-0 developmental checks

Developmental checks keep normal 5E total-vs-DC resolution:

1. roll the d20;
2. add the relevant modifier and any legitimately earned circumstance/proficiency modifier;
3. compare the total to the authored DC;
4. total meeting/exceeding the DC succeeds; a lower total fails.

From Zero to Hero then converts the result into development:

| Outcome | Growth |
| --- | --- |
| **Critical success** | **+2 Flexible Development Points** |
| **Success** | **+1 Flexible Development Point** |
| **Failure** | **+1 Lesson Point** locked to a different ability justified by what the character learned |
| **Critical failure** | **+1 Lesson Point** to a different ability, plus move **1 already-invested point** from the attempted ability into that lesson ability when possible |

A successful natural 20 is a critical success. A failed natural 1 is a critical failure. The extra critical labels are Level-0 house rules; they do not alter normal Level-1+ check rules.

### Why failure still matters

Failure is not "no advancement." The character develops, but the lesson can take them somewhere they did not intend.

Example: a child tries to solve a crisis with Strength and critically fails. The consequence teaches caution, observation, empathy, endurance, planning, or another ability justified by the scene. One already-invested STR point can move to that off-path ability, and the new Lesson Point is locked there as well.

This means a critical failure can visibly pull a future martial character away from Strength and toward Wisdom, Intelligence, Charisma, Constitution, or Dexterity without deleting the character's total earned development.

### Growth boundaries

- Flexible points may be spent on any ability below 15.
- Lesson Points may be spent only on their tagged ability.
- The lesson ability must differ from the attempted ability.
- Critical failure redirects at most one previously invested point per developmental check.
- No number of failures or critical failures can reduce the completed campaign below the 27-point graduation floor.
- If the attempted ability has no invested point to redirect, the critical failure still grants its Lesson Point but creates no negative point balance.
- Roll outcomes may change relationships, reputation, scars, possessions, clocks, and village state in addition to these growth effects.
- Failure must still fail forward; no result can brick the campaign.

## 5A. Training, familiarity, and proficiency

Level-0 training is recorded as **familiarity**, not automatic 5E proficiency.

A character can repeatedly practice Athletics, smithing, a weapon, a language, herbalism, arcane study, and so on. Those entries document who the character is becoming and may guide graduation choices.

In default **RAW Graduation** mode, familiarity adds no proficiency bonus by itself. At Level 1 it becomes mechanical only when a legal option from the selected edition grants it:
- 2014 race/background/class choices;
- 2024 species/background/class choices.

If the legal Level-1 package does not grant that proficiency, the familiarity remains narrative history. This prevents the Level-0 campaign from silently adding extra proficiencies to an otherwise RAW character.

## 5B. Challenge curve and Character Advantage

Base success should be uncommon. The authored DC floor rises as the characters grow so increasing ability scores do not make the mini-adventures trivial:

| Age | Minimum developmental DC |
| --- | ---: |
| 12–13 | 15 |
| 14–16 | 16 |
| 17–18 | 17 |

A player can earn **Character Advantage** when the attempted action is specifically grounded in the character or scene. Qualifying examples include:

- using an established bond, fear, value, aspiration, relationship, mentor lesson, familiarity, possession, or earlier consequence;
- exploiting a concrete scene feature or accepting a meaningful cost/risk that makes the approach more credible;
- clearly explaining what the character actually does.

Performance is never required. Accents, theatrical delivery, confidence, or player eloquence are not prerequisites.

The same generic justification cannot be repeated indefinitely. The Advantage must be specific to the current scene and the character's established history.

Character Advantage follows the normal Advantage procedure: roll two d20s and use the higher result. It never stacks with itself. The kept die is the natural result used by the Level-0 critical-success/critical-failure house rule.

Failure to earn Character Advantage does **not** impose Disadvantage. The player simply makes the normal hard check.

## 6. Growing-up sheet

The Level-0 sheet tracks:

- name
- ancestry/species
- chronological age
- ancestry-equivalent life stage
- family/guardians
- home/community
- childhood work/chores
- values/personality
- fears
- bonds
- friend and rival
- **What I am striving to become**
- all six scores and unspent progress toward the next point
- learned skill/tool/weapon/language familiarities
- training
- injuries/scars
- relationships
- reputation
- possessions
- important choices
- mentor candidates and mentor progress
- heirloom identity/state
- yearly milestone history
- discovered calling

Aspiration is controlled by the player and may change at any time. It is guidance, never a class lock.

## 7. Village contract

The starting village is a persistent campaign character.

It needs:
- recurring family/guardians
- peers, friends, rivals
- martial, arcane, divine, primal, and expert/social mentor paths
- authority figure
- healer
- crafter
- merchant/innkeeper
- troublemaker
- outsider
- evolving mystery NPC

Important NPCs need appearance, voice, wants, fears, secret, relationship state, what they can teach/provide, and how they change over the years.

The village must visibly evolve: marriages, deaths, departures, businesses, repairs, damage, promotions, aging mentors, grudges, and consequences of player choices.

## 8. Mini-adventure contract

Each yearly mini-adventure requires:
- active objective
- three Development Opportunities per character
- explicit DCs
- all four outcome bands
- multiple ability approaches
- at least one persistent NPC relationship beat
- at least one meaningful choice/consequence
- visible pressure/clock when useful
- fail-forward resolution
- concrete activity, not conversation alone

Combat is sparse. When combat occurs, it has a job beyond reducing HP and provides noncombatant/support roles for characters who are not developing martially.

## 8A. Life-first campaign rhythm

From Zero to Hero is **not seven conventional adventures scaled down for children**. The first half is ordinary village life with gameable problems.

The default seven-year rhythm is:

1. **Age 12 — Spring Chore Day:** shared household/community work, small mishaps, first adult trust.
2. **Age 13 — The Spring Dance:** setup, friendships, awkward social problems, celebration.
3. **Age 14 — Harvest Fair & Team Games:** competition, stalls, teamwork, handling victory or embarrassment.
4. **Age 15 — Apprentice Week:** mentors, crafts, real work, mistakes under supervision.
5. **Age 16 — The Road to Bellmere:** first unsupervised trip away from Hearthcross and first taste of the wider world.
6. **Age 17 — The Long Storm:** first serious community emergency where adults genuinely rely on the group.
7. **Age 18 — The Last Spring Dance:** coming-of-age celebration, unfinished relationships, then the final crisis/calling.

### Group-first rule

Every mini-adventure must give the whole party a natural reason to be together before the first roll. Do not create a single "main character" while the other youths watch.

Each chapter should include:
- a shared group responsibility;
- simultaneous or divisible jobs;
- room for all six ability approaches;
- social/relationship play as well as task play;
- at least one decision the group makes together;
- consequences that can persist into later years.

### Childhood danger rule

Ages **12–15** are ordinary-life chapters. They may contain accidents, embarrassment, weather, animals, broken equipment, arguments, lost property, or other believable complications, but they should not default to lethal combat, monster attacks, raids, or world-saving stakes.

Age **16** can introduce travel risk and independence but remains low-danger by default.

Age **17** is the first default serious community-danger chapter.

Age **18** may contain the origin campaign's true crisis because the characters are now at the threshold of Level 1.

The purpose is escalation by **life stage**, not escalation because every session needs a bigger villain.

## 8B. Shared Growth Beats, Yearbook, and continuity

### Every player gets the beat

Each of the three yearly Growth Beats is one **shared group situation**, not one check for the whole party.

Every player character receives exactly one Development Check inside each Growth Beat. The scene does not close because the first character succeeds.

Rules:
- no character receives a second Development Check in the beat until every character has had one;
- rotate who acts first so the same player does not always define the scene;
- helping another character is allowed, but assistance does not replace the helper's own Development Check;
- after each character acts, update the fiction so later characters address the new state instead of repeating an identical roll;
- an early success can improve the position, reduce consequences, or change what remains to be done, but cannot erase another player's Development Opportunity.

This is how a four-player group still gives **each character** all 21 base Development Opportunities across the seven-year campaign.

### Yearbook reflection

After the three Growth Beats resolve, each character records a short **Yearbook** entry before advancing to the next age.

Yearbook entries:
- grant **no Development Points**;
- cannot increase ability scores or proficiencies;
- record one concrete memory from the year;
- record a relationship that changed;
- confirm whether the character's aspiration stayed the same or changed;
- record a visible change in Hearthcross;
- may include the between-years seasonal memory.

A recorded Yearbook memory can later support Character Advantage only when it is genuinely relevant to the current attempt. Writing a memory does not create a reusable generic Advantage token.

### Between the years

The campaign includes six short interludes between ages 12 and 18:
- First Snow;
- High Summer;
- The Long Evenings;
- Choosing Where to Spend Your Time;
- Letters from Beyond Hearthcross;
- After the Storm.

These are ordinary-life prompts, not extra Development Opportunities. They exist to make time passing visible, deepen family/mentor/friend continuity, and let Hearthcross visibly change from year to year.

## 9. Graduation: 2014

Level-0 produces the **pre-origin base ability array**.

For 2014 graduation:
1. freeze the earned base array;
2. apply the selected legal 2014 species/race ability adjustments and traits;
3. apply the legal 2014 background proficiencies/equipment/features;
4. apply the selected certified Level-1 class package;
5. carry forward story identity, relationships, mentor, scars, reputation, and eligible learned proficiencies only where the graduation rules explicitly allow them;
6. do not import any 2024 origin feat/background mechanics.

## 10. Graduation: 2024

Level-0 produces the **pre-origin base ability array**.

For 2024 graduation:
1. freeze the earned base array;
2. apply the selected legal 2024 background ability-score increases;
3. apply the selected legal 2024 origin feat and background proficiencies/equipment;
4. apply legal species traits;
5. apply the selected certified Level-1 class package;
6. carry forward story identity, relationships, mentor, scars, reputation, and eligible learned proficiencies only where the graduation rules explicitly allow them;
7. do not import 2014 race/background mechanics.

## 11. Graduation boundary and optional Origin Legacy

From Zero to Hero ability generation is explicitly a **Level-0 house rule**.

At graduation the site audits the final base array:
- **below 27:** graduation is blocked; already-earned growth must be spent or settled until the floor is reached;
- **exactly 27:** the character has received the complete baseline development budget;
- **28–30:** visibly labeled **Heroic / house-rule ability generation**, normally caused by spent critical-success bonus points;
- **above 30:** invalid; the interface and validator must prevent this state;
- no pre-origin score may exceed 15.

Once the base array is frozen, the selected edition becomes authoritative.

For 2014, legal race/species traits and adjustments, background, class, HP, proficiencies, equipment, spells, feats, and later advancement must follow the 2014 ruleset.

For 2024, legal species, background ability adjustments, Origin feat, class, HP, proficiencies, equipment, spells, feats, and later advancement must follow the 2024 ruleset.

Training/familiarity does not become free proficiency. Mentors do not provide automatic numerical bonuses. An heirloom remains mundane/narrative unless a legal rule or explicitly enabled house rule grants it mechanics.

A future **Heroic Legacy** option may add a separate bounded reward, but it must be visibly labeled house rule and may never be confused with RAW graduation mechanics.

## 11A. Graduation keepsake packet

Completing the Level-0 campaign unlocks a printable **two-page graduation packet** after the character has a legal 27–30 base array and selects the 2014 or 2024 graduation path.

### Page 1 — Hearthcross Coming-of-Age Certificate

The certificate records:
- character name;
- Hearthcross as the home community;
- age range 12–18;
- ancestry/species identity as entered by the player;
- selected 2014 or 2024 graduation path;
- frozen Level-0 base ability array;
- final point-buy value;
- baseline vs Heroic/house-rule array label;
- final aspiration;
- critical-success count;
- signature lines for Player, Dungeon Master, Mentor/Guardian, and Hearthcross Witness.

Until certified Level-1 class conversion is actually wired, the certificate must say the character is **ready for certified Level-1 creation**. It may not claim a class, subclass, feat, proficiency, spell, or other Level-1 mechanic that has not been generated and certified.

### Page 2 — Origin Chronicle

The Origin Chronicle summarizes:
- family/guardians and childhood work;
- the seven Yearbook memories from ages 12–18;
- meaningful relationship and aspiration changes;
- final relationships, reputation/scars, values, and fears;
- training/familiarities;
- possessions/heirloom history;
- important choices;
- critical successes, successes, failures/hard lessons, critical failures, critical-bonus DP earned, and final point-buy value.

The Chronicle is a summary. The full Growing-Up Sheet and Yearbook remain the authoritative detailed history.

### Mechanical boundary

The certificate and Chronicle are **commemorative only**.

They grant no:
- feat;
- proficiency;
- ASI;
- spell;
- class feature;
- equipment entitlement;
- magic-item property;
- bonus Development Point;
- or any other mechanical benefit.

Printing, signing, or possessing a graduation keepsake can never change the character's rules state.

## 12. Safety and tone

The characters begin as adolescents. Early chapters should use age-appropriate stakes: family, community, accidents, weather, animals, mysteries, competitions, chores, rescue, social conflict, and limited danger.

Consequences can matter without making child death the normal failure state. Stakes and combat danger increase as the characters age.

## 13. Release gate

From Zero to Hero is not production-ready until:
- the data contract validates;
- all seven chapters exist and ages run 12 through 18;
- exactly 21 base Development Opportunities exist;
- every Growth Beat has an explicit DC, all six approaches, and four outcome bands;
- developmental DCs respect the age-band floors 15 / 16 / 17;
- Character Advantage is available only for specific fiction-grounded play, never acting skill;
- Advantage rolls two d20s and uses the higher result, does not stack, and records why it was earned;
- failure to earn Character Advantage never imposes Disadvantage;
- success awards +1 flexible point;
- failure awards +1 off-path Lesson Point;
- critical success awards +2 flexible points total;
- critical failure awards +1 off-path Lesson Point and redirects at most one already-invested point from the attempted ability;
- no ability exceeds 15 before edition-specific adjustments;
- the wallet may be spent between growth scenes;
- the site displays flexible, lesson, and critical-bonus growth separately;
- graduation is impossible below a 27-point base-array value after all 21 base opportunities;
- Maturity Settlement can recover only stranded/cap-blocked earned growth and cannot mint new points;
- the final base array is audited and clearly labeled if it exceeds 27 because of critical-success bonus growth;
- no Development Point investment may raise the final base array above 30;
- familiarity cannot silently become proficiency;
- 2014 and 2024 graduation mechanics stay isolated;
- the custom sheet persists and prints;
- graduation unlocks a two-page certificate + Origin Chronicle packet only after a valid 27–30 array and edition selection;
- graduation keepsakes remain explicitly commemorative and grant zero mechanical benefit;
- the certificate cannot claim an unwired Level-1 class or mechanic;
- no Level-0 critical rule leaks into ordinary Level-1+ checks.
