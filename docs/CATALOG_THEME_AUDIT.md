# Catalog Theme Audit

**Status: audited and repaired curated-catalog design map — 2026-10-05**

Purpose: every One-Shot Forge product should be recognizable from a one-sentence theme spine and should pay that idea off through its actual play, not only its title or flavor text.

## The Lantern Below
**Theme spine:** A village blessing is actually a prison, and the thief may have stolen the key for the right reason.

**Keep on path:** dead oil-less lantern → stolen Emberglass → evidence the seal is failing → Caldus's motive → Pale Guest truth → choose what the seal becomes.

**Memorable turn:** the apparent theft becomes an attempted rescue/rebinding.

**Payoff:** destroy, bind, bargain, or restore the same object/problem introduced in the hook.

## The Well That Would Not Dry
**Theme spine:** A well gives water by taking names, and somebody broke the old bargain on purpose.

**Keep on path:** name loss → displaced name-stone → keeper's guilt → Draught's terms → dusk bucket.

**Memorable turn:** the curse is not random haunting; it is infrastructure with a price.

**Payoff:** restore, trick, pay, break, or abandon the bargain.

## The Owlbear Egg
**Theme spine:** Hunger turns a stolen living egg into a moral resource dispute while its parents come looking.

**Keep on path:** starvation → stolen egg → three arguments → father arrives → fake druid exposed → egg decision → mother consequence.

**Memorable turn:** killing the first threat can make the family problem worse instead of solving it.

**Payoff:** no moral answer is scored as correct; the family/village consequence reflects the party's own decision.

## Birthday One Shot
**Theme spine:** The real birthday person becomes the hero of a stolen-wish adventure built around their celebration.

**Keep on path:** named birthday hero → stolen wish → celebration-hall obstacles → thief's reason → midnight choice.

**Memorable turn:** the personalization is not decoration; the table is literally rescuing this person's birthday wish.

**Payoff:** the finale resolves the wish/celebration problem, not an unrelated cosmic abstraction.

## Whispers of the Oakhaven Hollow
**Theme spine:** A druid tried to save his grove by taking its corruption into himself, and the party must decide whether he can still be saved.

**Keep on path:** failing Gloom-Lilies → corrupted grove → bear can be freed → Decay/Balance/Growth shrine → Malakor's lucid fragments → cleanse or kill.

**Memorable turn:** the "boss" is also the person who made the first sacrificial attempt to save the place.

**Payoff:** the same cycle used in the shrine becomes the emotional logic of restoring the grove and Malakor.

## Beneath the Eastern Watchtower
**Theme spine:** Oakhaven's failing civic ward can be repaired under pressure or destroyed at the known cost of the watchtower above it.

**Keep on path:** underground failure → pressure puzzle → three repair jobs → rising strain → deliberate destructive shortcut.

**Memorable turn:** the emergency escape is legal and effective, but the cost is known before the choice.

**Payoff:** repair the system or sacrifice the tower; no surprise punishment afterward.

## The Black Orchard
**Theme spine:** One living wardstone can protect Oakhaven or preserve the orchard that is already protecting everyone from something worse.

**Keep on path:** missing surveyor → strange fruit → containment truth → loosened shard → two legitimate communities → living-graft third way.

**Memorable turn:** the "resource" is part of a living containment system, so taking it creates the next crisis.

**Payoff:** return it, take it, or attempt to grow a third solution.

## The Ashen Bridge
**Theme spine:** A collapsing bridge forces the party to prove what they value when lives, medicine, cargo, and infrastructure cannot all be treated equally.

**Keep on path:** trapped caravan → visible integrity → load choices → ash-lime repair → live evacuation.

**Memorable turn:** saving every person is explicitly success even if the structure is lost.

**Payoff:** the final board state tells the story: who lived, what survived, and what Oakhaven lost.

## The Bell Beneath the Mere
**Theme spine:** A drowned ghost is not luring victims—she is still sounding a warning the town's founders chose not to hear.

**Keep on path:** underwater bell → missing apprentice → rising water → LOW/HIGH/LOW warning → Ysra's duty → founder diversion truth.

**Memorable turn:** the haunting changes from threat to testimony.

**Payoff:** rescue Tamsin, answer the warning, then decide whether Ysra's bell is released, silenced, or preserved.

## Right to OwlBear Arms
**Theme spine:** A genius owlbear has a legitimate legal claim to the village's weapons while a raid deadline makes the argument immediately practical.

**Keep on path:** property dispute → evidence/negotiation → preparation → sundown pressure → defense consequences.

**Memorable turn:** the absurd legal premise is treated seriously enough that the players can actually argue the case.

**Payoff:** the law and the coming raid collide; negotiation choices must matter in the defense.

## From Zero to Hero: The Hearthcross Years
**Theme spine:** Children become Level-1 heroes because seven years of ordinary village life teach them who they are before the world asks them to act.

**Keep on path:** chores → dances → fairs → apprenticeship → first journey → storm → final spring → Watchstone crisis → graduation.

**Memorable turn:** character creation is the adventure rather than paperwork before the adventure.

**Payoff:** the final sheet and scrapbook visibly come from the player's own seven-year history.

## Cross-catalog uniqueness check

Two products are too similar if they share most of these:
- central object/problem;
- primary clock;
- twist type;
- finale procedure;
- moral choice;
- emotional relationship;
- physical environment;
- visual presentation rhythm.

Reuse **presentation clarity**, not adventure identity.

Approved reference adventures are used for:
- immediate hook;
- clean scene flow;
- readable cards/pages;
- concrete checks and dimensions;
- visible pressure;
- explicit puzzle solutions;
- useful NPC wants/voice;
- clean pacing/cut guidance;
- finales that reconnect to the hook.

They are never used as plot banks, room-sequence templates, or sources of distinctive names/creatures/items/dialogue.


## The Fencewalker of Bellmere
**Theme spine:** The cryptid blamed for stealing Bellmere's iron is actually using it to warn and protect the road from collapse.

**Keep on path:** missing fence iron → impossible rail tracks → three-knock warning → stolen iron bracing the fissure → storm repair → choose road, property, and cryptid fate.

**Memorable turn:** the party catches the thief in the act and realizes the stolen property is already holding the village's road together.

**Payoff:** the finale asks what to save using the same iron theft and warning behavior introduced in the opening scene.

## Audit resolution — 2026-10-05

The original catalog review findings have been applied to product/source data:

- **Setting continuity:** Hearthcross, Hearthford, and Oakhaven are explicitly one region.
- **Lantern:** public hook uses the morally ambiguous thief premise; authoritative data now explains why the lantern never needed oil.
- **Well:** the name-stone is explicitly the broken component of the name-for-water bargain.
- **Owlbear Egg:** narrator-level sales copy treats the situation as survival ethics; omelet humor remains only as character/DM texture.
- **Birthday:** the real birthday person is explicitly the in-world honoree; Sepulchral's finale is grounded in his own failed birthday wish rather than abstractly putting "hope itself" on trial.
- **Whispers:** Malakor has recognizable memory, habits, motive, regret, lucid dialogue, and an explicit reason to cleanse him.
- **Watchtower:** Edda Marr and Harn's Echo have explicit wants that drive the repair choice; the destructive shortcut's cost remains known in advance.
- **Black Orchard:** the two-community/one-resource dilemma is foregrounded; Vessa, Lio, and Mother Graft actively want incompatible outcomes.
- **Ashen Bridge:** people-over-infrastructure remains the explicit success hierarchy; Soren and Mera's wants drive the rescue.
- **Bell Beneath the Mere:** Ysra's warning and the founders' wrongdoing remain the capstone's emotional engine.
- **Right to OwlBear Arms:** v1.2 resolves chronology, preparation, defense procedure, encounter math, table rules, custom items, hero completeness, and reveal timing while preserving the legal-procedural comedy premise.
- **From Zero to Hero:** the Hearthcross Six are introduced as specific children with families, fears, ambitions, relationships, and likely directions before the mechanics.

Future audit work should test these implementations for table feel and print/layout quality rather than reopening the resolved premise problems without new evidence.
