# One-Shot Forge World & Narrative Standard

**Status: OWNER-APPROVED PRODUCT STANDARD — 2026-10-05**

This document preserves the shared-world and story-quality decisions for the curated One-Shot Forge catalog. It does not override the ForgeBones/AI separation in `docs/FORGE_CONSTITUTION.md`; it governs curated setting continuity, public-facing hooks, and narrative quality.

## 1. Shared regional world

Hearthcross, Hearthford, and Oakhaven are not unrelated anthology settings. They exist in the same playable region.

- **Hearthcross** — a small rural village and the home of *From Zero to Hero*. This is where characters can grow up and build long-term relationships.
- **Hearthford** — the larger nearby market/river town and hub for *The Quiet Seals*.
- **Oakhaven** — a settlement and woodland region farther along the old road, home to the Oakhaven adventure sequence.

Default public-facing geographic relationship:

> Hearthcross lies about a day's ride from Hearthford, while the old road continues east toward Oakhaven.

Exact travel time may vary when an adventure gives a more specific route. Do not silently contradict an authored adventure package.

The product promise is that **the world remembers the players**. When useful and non-disruptive, consequences, rumors, merchants, NPC references, scars, favors, and prior decisions may cross between these places.

## 2. Catalog story test

Before an authored adventure is called narratively complete, it must answer:

1. **Who needs something?**
2. **What happens if the heroes do nothing?**
3. **What truth is initially hidden?**
4. **What meaningful choice cannot be solved simply by killing something?**
5. **What will the world remember afterward?**

The answers belong in authoritative adventure data when they affect story truth or consequences. Public catalog copy should communicate enough of them to sell the situation without spoiling the adventure.

## 3. Consequence standard

Use *Beneath the Eastern Watchtower* as the model for consequential choices:

- players should understand the meaningful cost before committing when the fiction reasonably allows it;
- consequences should be choices, not post-hoc gotchas;
- failure should move play forward;
- people, places, resources, relationships, and reputation can all carry persistent consequences.

## 4. Moral-choice voice

Preferred language is direct and non-judgmental.

> No moral answer is scored as correct.

Do not imply that the adventure has a hidden author-approved moral answer when multiple endings are intentionally legal.

## 5. Character-driven pressure

Systems, puzzles, hazards, and clocks become stronger when at least one person or creature actively wants something.

For major curated adventures, identify a want-driven character whenever practical:
- what they want now;
- what they fear losing;
- what they will do if the party refuses;
- what changes if the party helps or harms them.

## 6. Current catalog directives

### The Lantern Below
- Lead with the stronger moral hook: the thief may have taken the Emberglass for the right reason.
- Preserve the mystery of the oil-less lantern, but ensure the DM gets an early concrete payoff for why it matters.

### The Well That Would Not Dry
- Explicitly bridge the water curse and the name-stone so they read as one premise, not two unrelated devices.

### The Owlbear Egg
- Primary tone: serious survival ethics with occasional human humor.
- Comedy may come from character voice, but must not trivialize starvation, grief, dead parents, or the living egg.
- Preserve: “No moral answer is scored as correct.”

### Birthday One Shot
- Personalization is the headline feature, not a buried bullet.
- Be explicit that the real birthday person’s name becomes the in-adventure birthday hero/honoree.
- Keep the finale emotionally proportional to the playful premise; avoid abstract stakes that dwarf the cake/wish setup.

### Whispers of the Oakhaven Hollow
- Malakor must be a person worth saving, not only a corrupted combat state.
- Preserve clear motive, memory, regret, recognizable habits, and evidence that some part of him remains underneath the corruption.

### Beneath the Eastern Watchtower
- Preserve the explicit, known cost of destructive success.
- Treat this as the catalog reference for foreshadowed consequences.

### The Black Orchard
- Feature the two-community/one-resource dilemma prominently.
- Do not undersell it as only “Module 04.”

### The Ashen Bridge
- Preserve the stated values hierarchy: saving every person counts as success even if the bridge is lost.
- Keep civilians, integrity, cargo, and wagon pressure mechanically visible.

### The Bell Beneath the Mere
- Preserve the founders’ wrongdoing reveal and Ysra’s active desire to warn.
- Use its want-driven character structure as a reference when improving other Oakhaven entries.

### Right to OwlBear Arms
- Treat as a high-priority flagship comedy adventure.
- Preserve its legal-procedural voice: the law is unclear; the claws are not.
- Fix story chronology/rules debt without sanding away the premise.

### From Zero to Hero
- The six starter children are characters, not roster labels.
- Their family, aspiration, flaw/fear, relationships, and likely heroic direction should be visible early enough for players to care who grows up.
- The scrapbook is meaningful because the child already has an identity before the first growth beat.

## 7. Public-copy rule

If the authoritative package contains a stronger hook than the catalog card, the catalog card is wrong.

Product-page copy should foreground:
- the immediate problem;
- the human/creature want;
- the unusual choice;
- the visible cost or clock;
- the distinct reason to choose this adventure.

Do not replace specific hooks with generic phrases such as “make a difficult choice” when the actual choice can be stated more sharply.

## 8. Anti-drift rule

Before changing curated adventure story structure or storefront copy, read:
1. `docs/FORGE_CONSTITUTION.md`
2. `docs/WORLD_AND_NARRATIVE_STANDARD.md`
3. the authoritative adventure package
4. the relevant public product page

Do not invent a replacement premise from the title alone. Preserve authored truth, then improve how clearly it is expressed.
