From Zero to Hero: The Hearthcross Years

A seven-session Level-0 origin campaign for one DM and 3–6 players. Start at age 12 with six 9s and no class. Grow up through chores, dances, competitions, work, travel, danger, and the night Hearthcross finally needs you.

THE HEARTHCROSS SIX

They are children before they are character builds.

Each starter already has a family, fear, ambition, relationships, and a reason to care about Hearthcross. The seven-year campaign does not invent their identity from scratch—it records how that identity changes under pressure.

Tod HaleBig for his age and dependable to a fault. He wants to be the person everyone calls when something difficult needs doing, but fears being valued only for his strength.
Merlon QuillA quiet puzzle-hunter from a house of ledgers and old maps. He wants to discover what nobody else understands and fears being ordinary more than he admits.
Halley BrindleQuick, practical, and raised in the market. She wants freedom enough that nobody can decide where she has to stay, and hates needing help she cannot guarantee will come.
Mira AlderPatient and trusted around animals and injuries. She wants to become the person who keeps others alive when everyone else panics, but fears making the wrong call when it matters.
Fen BellAlways outside and always listening. He wants to know every road beyond Bellmere and come home with everyone he left with.
Lark CaskA born performer who can turn chores into a show. Lark wants to be remembered, but is already learning that making people laugh and caring for them are not always the same thing.

Hearthcross lies about a day’s ride from Hearthford; the old road continues east toward Oakhaven. A child who graduates here can step into the same wider region the one-shot catalog keeps changing.

10-MINUTE FIRST-SESSION SETUP

Three steps. Then read the opening aloud.

  1. Choose the children

    Use Tod, Merlon, Halley, and Mira for four players. For three, pick any three of them (the fourth stays on as a village friend). Add Fen and Lark for five or six.

    Every ability starts at 9. Keep each child's race/species, choose 2014 or 2024, and confirm their Upbringing Lean: 2 Favored, 2 Neutral, 2 Challenging. Class stays blank.

    Open the six starter sheets

  2. Give out the materials

    Each player needs a Growth Sheet to record checks and a journal for memories.

    Put the player map on the table. Keep the DM map hidden. Print awards when you reach the Harvest Fair.

    Keep the quick rules beside you

  3. Start Spring Chore Day

    Open the DM runner. Read “Open the session,” then “Read this to the players.” Follow the numbered steps.

    Every player gets one Development Check in each Growth Beat. Record the result on that player's Growth Sheet. Continue when “Move on when” is satisfied.

    Run the first session

THE RULES IN ONE MINUTE

What a first-time buyer must know

Start equal

Every ability begins at 9. Race/species is identity; class is not chosen yet.

Upbringing changes odds

Each child has 2 Favored abilities, 2 Neutral, and 2 Challenging. Favored rolls with Advantage; Challenging with Disadvantage.

Play builds the sheet

Each of 21 Growth Beats creates a Development Card and development result.

Failure still builds you

Failure gives a Lesson Point instead of deleting growth. Critical failure can redirect where one invested point landed.

Bounded ending

Everyone who completes the campaign reaches at least 27 point-buy value and can never exceed 30.

After Level 0

Freeze the finished scores, then build Level 1 using the character's chosen 2014 or 2024 rules. Automated, certified Level-1 conversion is still in development.
